Week Ahead — Catalyst Map

MONDAY SEP 14 – FRIDAY SEP 18, 2026 · PUBLISHED SUNDAY SEP 13
This is the decision the whole quarter has been arguing toward. The FOMC decides Wednesday with futures near 70% for a 25bp hike under Chair Warsh — up from 44% a month ago — and the inflation pair that was supposed to settle it did not. PPI's annual rate accelerated to 5.4% on Thursday; CPI landed in line Friday at +0.4% and 3.4%, core 2.4%. Both said the same thing: energy is doing the inflating — gasoline +27.4% year-over-year, over a third of CPI's monthly increase, diesel at a record near $6 — which is precisely the kind of inflation a rate hike cannot fix. Then Monday's Hormuz talks could take the oil price down without touching any of it.
BIGGEST CATALYST OF THE QUARTER
WED 9/16 — FOMC DECISION
DECISION PM · WARSH PRESS CONFERENCE
Futures price roughly 70% odds of a 25bp hike, and the two-day meeting starts Tuesday. Warsh committed at Jackson Hole to bringing inflation down and BofA counted 61 inflation references against 30 labor in that speech; Waller said he could back a hold unless August inflation ran hot. It ran in line, which resolved nothing — and the 2-year sits at its highest in over two years with the 10-year near 4.95% and the 30-year around 5.36%. The tell to watch is not the decision but the press conference: the energy impulse is the part a hike does not address, and whether Warsh says so is the whole information content.
THE WEEK'S FIRST HEADLINE
MON 9/14 — HORMUZ TALKS
GULF MINISTERS MEET IRAN
Gulf foreign ministers are reportedly aiming to meet Iran on reopening the Strait. That report alone took WTI back under $100 from Thursday's $108 Brent print and knocked USO 2.23% on Friday — before any meeting happened. Every energy setup this book carried last week named this as the single headline that ends the trade, and it is now dated. The complication is that nothing physical has changed: Houthis hold Mokha, the IEA calls refining “stretched to the limit”, and Bessent has warned a “large bank” faces sanction this week over its role helping Iran. A meeting is not a reopening.
WHERE THE POSITIONING EXPIRES
FRI 9/18 — QUAD WITCHING
TWO DAYS AFTER THE DECISION
Both of the largest options tickets on Thursday's board expire this Friday, and neither was positioned for last week: a $17.5M ECHO $130P floor block and a $1.2M TXN $260C sweep, both dated 09/18 — through the Fed decision and onto quad witching. That is size deliberately carrying the FOMC. Quarterly expiry two sessions after a live rate decision concentrates a great deal of mechanical flow into one session, and it is the reason a clean post-FOMC trend can look very different by Friday's close.

How to Trade the Shape of This Week

A hike is priced; a hold is not. That asymmetry is the trade. Futures sit near 70% for 25bp, which means the hike is largely in the price and the surprise lives on the other side. The reaction to an expected hike is usually about the press conference rather than the decision — specifically whether Warsh acknowledges that the inflation showing up in the data is an energy impulse that policy does not reach. Positioning for the decision itself is positioning for the outcome the market already holds.

The two inflation prints disagreed about urgency and agreed about cause. PPI's annual rate accelerated to 5.4% with energy +4.2% and diesel +24.1%. CPI came in line at +0.4% and 3.4% with core at 2.4%, food +0.1% and shelter +0.3%. Read together: the underlying trend is behaving and the pump is doing the inflating, with gasoline +27.4% year-over-year accounting for over a third of CPI's monthly increase. That is the case for a hold and the reason a hike may not help, in the same two numbers.

Do not let Monday's oil headline price Wednesday's decision. A Hormuz reopening lowers the oil price. It does not remove the energy already in the August data the Fed is deciding on. Last week proved how fast this moves in the other direction too — Brent went from under $100 to over $108 and back under $100 inside four sessions. Energy was the only bid for three straight days and then closed red on Thursday with Brent up 6%, which is when it stopped being a sector story and became a rates story.

Size for Friday, not just for Wednesday. Quad witching lands two sessions after a live decision, and the board's two largest options tickets are dated to it. A clean directional move on Wednesday can be substantially unwound by Friday's close through mechanical expiry flow that carries no information. Anything held from the decision through expiry is carrying two distinct risks, and only one of them is the Fed.
