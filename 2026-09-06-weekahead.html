Week Ahead — Catalyst Map

MONDAY SEP 7 – FRIDAY SEP 11, 2026 · PUBLISHED SUNDAY SEP 6 · UPDATED MON SEP 7
Four sessions, and the last one decides the Fed. Labor Day is Monday Sept 7 — markets closed. Friday's payrolls came in at 162,000 against a 56,000 consensus, July revised from −23k to +21k, and the 2-year snapped from 4.34% to 4.425% within seconds. August CPI Friday 7:30am settles it — core seen holding at 2.5% — the last inflation print before the Sept 15-16 FOMC. Between here and there: Apple's iPhone event Wednesday noon CT, Oracle and Adobe Thursday post-close, an ECB decision Thursday morning, and the NFL season opener. And the tape is dangerously narrow — Friday closed SPY −0.38% with eight of twelve sectors red while SMH added 2.60%.
BIGGEST CATALYST OF THE WEEK
FRI 9/11 — AUGUST CPI
7:30AM CT · LAST INPUT BEFORE THE FOMC
Economists expect core to hold at 2.5%, with the airfare component flagged for another notable increase. Payrolls already did their part — 162k against 56k — but this was always the swing input: Waller said he supports a hold “if there is continued progress” and would “consider a rate hike” if inflation runs hot, and BofA counted 61 inflation references against 30 labor in Warsh's Jackson Hole speech. Note the index gap this creates: core CPI at 2.5% sits well under the 3.3% core PCE Waller actually targets, so a benign CPI headline does not automatically satisfy his condition. Watch GLD first — it broke 408.54 on the jobs print, rallied to 408.00 Friday and stopped fifty-four cents short.
SINGLE-NAME EVENT OF THE WEEK
WED 9/9 — APPLE, 12:00PM CT
TIME NOW CONFIRMED · FIRST LAUNCH UNDER TERNUS
Morgan Stanley previews it as possibly Apple's most consequential launch since iPhone X: the first foldable plus the largest iPhone price increases in years, which makes it a direct test of pricing power rather than a spec refresh. Two things cut against it. AAPL fell 2.50% Friday to 319.99 heading into its own event, and Nikkei reported foldable production limited to a few hundred units a day — a supply constraint already public before the keynote, which caps the upside surprise. The supplier chain moves on unit guidance, not on the presentation.
THE HEAVIEST MACRO DAY
THU 9/10 — ORCL, ADBE, PPI & THE ECB
ECB 7:15AM · PPI 7:30AM · BOTH REPORTS POST-CLOSE
Four events stacked. August PPI is the producer-side warm-up that moves Friday's CPI expectations before CPI lands. The ECB policy statement at 7:15am with Lagarde at 7:45am is the piece most likely to be underpriced — eurozone inflation at 3.3% has the market expecting a hike, and a global duration move is exactly what drove 9/1. Then ORCL post-close, the September AI-capex checkpoint with cloud growth guided north of 55%, closing Friday +3.07% at 158.77; and ADBE, reporting days after naming Anil Chakravarthy CEO, having fallen 6.74% to 266.50 on that news.

How to Trade the Shape of This Week

Everything before Friday is positioning, not resolution. The FOMC is split 50/50 and CPI is the stated tiebreaker. Monday is closed, Tuesday is conferences, and Wednesday and Thursday are single-name events. Nothing between now and 7:30am Friday changes the rate question — which means size taken earlier in the week is being carried into a binary, across a shortened week, whether or not that was the intent.

The ECB on Thursday is the underpriced event on this page. Eurozone inflation has run to 3.3% and the market expects a hike, with the statement at 7:15am CT and Lagarde at 7:45am — forty-five minutes before and fifteen after August PPI. The 9/1 session is the precedent worth remembering: that selloff was not domestic. It was a global duration repricing, with the JGB through 3.00% for the first time since 1996 and European 10-years up ~10bp, and it cost SMH 2.05% in a single session. A hawkish ECB into a hot PPI is the same setup.

Apple is a launch into weakness, and the constraint is already public. AAPL fell 2.50% Friday into its own event week, and the foldable supply limit — a few hundred units a day, per Nikkei — was reported before the keynote. Pre-announced constraints cap the upside surprise, which is what separates this from a normal launch. The reliable expression has usually been the supplier chain on unit guidance rather than the stock on the presentation, and the largest price increases in years make it a pricing-power test where the answer arrives in the December quarter, not on Wednesday.

The index went nowhere last week; the semis did all of it — on the day that should have hurt them most. SPY finished the week +0.11%, and Friday itself closed −0.38% with eight of twelve sectors red while SMH added 2.60% — SNDK +11.90%, MU +5.93% through $1,000, AMD +4.69%, AMAT +4.31%. That happened on a 162k payroll beat that flattened the curve in seconds, which is not the textbook response for the longest-duration complex on the board. Either AI capex is being priced as rate-insensitive, or Friday was positioning that unwinds on the first bad print. ORCL Thursday tests the first, CPI Friday tests the second.
