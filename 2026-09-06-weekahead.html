Week Ahead — Catalyst Map

MONDAY SEP 7 – FRIDAY SEP 11, 2026 · PUBLISHED SUNDAY SEP 6
Four sessions, and the last one decides the Fed. Labor Day is Monday Sept 7 — markets closed. Friday's payrolls came in at 162,000 against a 56,000 consensus, with July revised from −23k to +21k — the 2-year yield snapped from 4.34% to 4.425% within seconds and the curve flattened. That raised the odds; August CPI on Friday 7:30am settles them, the last inflation print before the Sept 15-16 FOMC. Waller named his condition out loud: he backs a hold unless August inflation runs hot. Apple launches the iPhone 18 Wednesday, Oracle reports Thursday. And the tape is dangerously narrow — Friday closed SPY −0.38% with eight of twelve sectors red while SMH added 2.60% and MU broke $1,000.

How to Trade the Shape of This Week

Everything before Friday is positioning, not resolution. The FOMC is split 50/50 and CPI is the stated tiebreaker. Monday is closed, Tuesday is empty, and Wednesday and Thursday are single-name events. Nothing between now and 7:30am Friday changes the rate question — which means size taken earlier in the week is being carried into a binary, across a shortened week, whether or not that was the intent.

Watch gold, not the index, for the inflation read. Friday's jobs print gave the template: GLD fell from 409.88 to 402.35 in three minutes while SPY moved a fifth of a percent. Gold broke Thursday's 408.54 low and never reclaimed it, closing the week at 406.75. Gold selling on hot data is a real-yield move rather than a growth scare, and it prices the Fed faster and more legibly than the index does.

Apple is a launch into weakness, which is a different trade. AAPL fell 2.50% on Friday into its own event week, and the foldable supply constraint — a few hundred units a day, per Nikkei — is already public. Pre-announced constraints cap the upside surprise, and the reliable expression of an Apple launch has usually been the supplier chain on unit guidance rather than the keynote itself.

The index went nowhere last week; the semis did all of it — on the day that should have hurt them most. SPY finished the week +0.11%, and Friday itself closed −0.38% with eight of twelve sectors red while SMH added 2.60% — SNDK +11.90%, MU +5.93% through $1,000, AMD +4.69%, AMAT +4.31%. That happened on a 162k payroll beat that flattened the curve in seconds, which is not the textbook response for the longest-duration complex on the board. Either AI capex is being priced as rate-insensitive, or Friday was positioning that unwinds on the first bad print. ORCL Thursday tests the first, CPI Friday tests the second. Meanwhile an index hedge into CPI is not a hedge on what is actually making money.
